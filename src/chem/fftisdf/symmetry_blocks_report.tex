\section*{What this note is about}

This summarizes how the pieces of the space-group change-of-basis $Q$ (the transform taking the
ISDF grid index $(k,I)$ to symmetry-adapted labels $(i,a,c)$) are related to one another, and how
little \emph{genuinely independent} information $Q$ contains. The running example is diamond
($n_{\mathrm{IP}}=136$ interpolation points per cell, $48$ point-group operations), on $2\times2\times2$
and $6\times6\times6$ $k$-meshes. Everything below is nonsymmorphic-safe: the lattice-vector /
fractional-translation phases are carried explicitly, and at Brillouin-zone boundaries the
little-group representations are the projective ones.

\section{Block structure of $Q$}

$Q$ is a square unitary of size $N=n_k\,n_{\mathrm{IP}}$. Two structural facts:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Block-diagonal in $k$.} Each symmetry-adapted vector has a definite momentum, so
        $Q=\bigoplus_k Q^{(k)}$ with $n_k$ blocks, each $n_{\mathrm{IP}}\times n_{\mathrm{IP}}$.
        $Q$ never mixes different momenta.
  \item \textbf{Grouped into families (stars).} The point-group operations permute the $k$-points
        into \emph{families}; within a family the members are carried into one another by
        ``move-it'' operations. Only \textbf{one block per family} is independent.
\end{enumerate}

Within a representative block $Q^{(k_0)}$, the transform is a direct sum of small little-group
``pattern'' blocks, one per interpolation-point orbit (size $\le |L_k|\le 48$).

\section{How the blocks of a family are related}

Fix a family with representative momentum $k_0$ and another member $k_j=g_j\cdot k_0$, where $g_j$ is
a chosen move-it operation. For any symmetry-adapted column $c$, the entries of the two blocks are
related \textbf{entry-by-entry} by

\[
  \boxed{\;Q^{(k_j)}_{\,\mathrm{perm}[g_j,I],\ c}
     \;=\; e^{-\,i\,k_j\cdot L_I(g_j)}\;\,Q^{(k_0)}_{\,I,\ c}\;}
\]

i.e.\ as matrices $\;Q^{(k_j)} = P(g_j)\,\Lambda(g_j,k_j)\,Q^{(k_0)}\;$, where $P(g_j)$ permutes the
interpolation-point rows by $\mathrm{perm}[g_j,\cdot]$ and $\Lambda$ is the diagonal Bloch phase
$e^{-i\,k_j\cdot L_I(g_j)}$. Reading it off, across a family and for the same column $c$:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{rows are permuted}: $I\mapsto \mathrm{perm}[g_j,I]$ (the grid shuffle of $g_j$);
  \item \textbf{each entry gets a phase} $e^{-i\,k_j\cdot L_I(g_j)}$ --- the only $k$-dependence;
  \item \textbf{magnitudes are unchanged}: $|Q^{(k_j)}_{\mathrm{perm}[g_j,I],c}|=|Q^{(k_0)}_{I,c}|$;
  \item \textbf{the pattern label $c$ is preserved} (same irrep, same little-group partner, same
        copy); only the star-member advances $k_0\to k_j$.
\end{itemize}

Here $L_I(g)$ is the \textbf{fold-back lattice vector}, defined by
$g\cdot r_I = r_{\mathrm{perm}[g,I]} + L_I(g)$ (for nonsymmorphic $g=\{R\mid\tau\}$ the fractional
translation $\tau$ is included in $g\cdot r_I$). It depends only on $(g,I)$, \emph{not} on $k$; the
$k$-dependence is entirely the computable phase $e^{-ik\cdot L}$. Different families are independent
(each keeps its own representative block).

\section{Amount of unique information}

Because every non-representative block is a phased-permutation \emph{image} of its family
representative, the genuinely independent content of $Q$ is just the representative blocks:

\[
  \#\{\text{unique numbers}\}\;=\;\sum_{\text{family reps}}\ \sum_{\text{orbits }o}|o|^2 .
\]

The auxiliary objects $g_j$ (which operation reaches which member) and $L_I(g)$ are \textbf{not}
per-family data: they are properties of the $48$ operations and the grid geometry, shared across all
families and all $k$. In fact they follow from a tiny, $k$-independent \textbf{generation kernel}:

\[
  \underbrace{n_{\mathrm{IP}}\times 3}_{\text{point positions}}
  \;+\;\underbrace{48\times(9+3)}_{\text{ops }(R,\tau)}
  \;\approx\; 1000\ \text{numbers},
\]

from which $\mathrm{perm}$, $L$, all phases, and the $k$-map are computed. Per family one records
\emph{only} its representative block.

\subsection*{The numbers, and how they scale}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
 & $2\times2\times2$ & $6\times6\times6$ & growth \\
\midrule
$n_k$ (momenta) & $8$ & $216$ & $27\times$ \\
families (stars) & $3$ & $16$ & $5.3\times$ \\
\textbf{unique data of $Q$} $\big(\sum|o|^2\big)$ & $\mathbf{6{,}800}$ & $\mathbf{12{,}912}$ & $\mathbf{1.9\times}$ \\
block-diagonal-in-$k$ \ $n_k\,n_{\mathrm{IP}}^2$ & $147{,}968$ & $3{,}995{,}136$ & $27\times\ (\propto n_k)$ \\
dense \ $(n_k\,n_{\mathrm{IP}})^2$ & $1{,}183{,}744$ & $862{,}949{,}376$ & $729\times\ (\propto n_k^2)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Key point.} From $2\times2\times2$ to $6\times6\times6$ the number of momenta grows $27\times$,
but the genuine unique data grows only $\approx 1.9\times$. It tracks the number of \emph{families},
not $n_k$ --- and even that sublinearly, because the many new $k$-points are low-symmetry (little
groups of size $2$ or $4$), whose small little groups split the $136$ points into many tiny orbits
that contribute almost nothing to $\sum|o|^2$ (the bulk is still the few high-symmetry families, e.g.\
$\Gamma$ alone gives $3872$). For $6\times6\times6$ the unique data ($12{,}912$) is about
$67{,}000\times$ smaller than the dense matrix ($8.6\times10^8$).

\section*{Summary}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $Q$ is block-diagonal in $k$; its blocks come in \textbf{families}, and only one block per
        family is independent.
  \item Within a family, member blocks are \textbf{phased-permutation images} of the representative:
        same magnitudes, rows permuted by $g_j$, times a member-specific Bloch phase
        $e^{-ik_j\cdot L}$; the pattern label is preserved.
  \item The unique information is $\sum_{\text{families}}\sum_{\text{orbits}}|o|^2$
        ($6{,}800$ for $2\times2\times2$, $12{,}912$ for $6\times6\times6$), plus a
        $\sim\!1000$-number, $k$-independent generation kernel. The $k$-dependence lives only in
        computable phases, so the information is \textbf{essentially flat in $n_k$} --- it scales with
        the number of families, not the number of $k$-points.
\end{itemize}
